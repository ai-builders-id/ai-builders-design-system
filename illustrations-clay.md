# AI Builders Clay Illustrations — Art Direction

## Creative Thesis

Turn precise digital concepts into objects that feel shaped, pressed, and arranged by hand.
Clay is the brand's tactile focal layer: warm, approachable, and materially believable without
becoming childish, mascot-led, or glossy claymorphism.

The signature tension is **recognizable structure versus handmade matter**. Preserve the
subject's silhouette and logic; introduce humanity through selective deformation and motion.

## Material Signature

- Dense reusable adhesive putty / plasticine, matte with faintly waxy light falloff.
- Shallow partial fingerprints, broad thumb dents, pinched edges, and compressed sides.
- Apply visible hand marks to selected areas only; untouched areas remain softly uneven.
- Forms hold their silhouettes and never slump, melt, crack, crumble, or look wet.
- Never drift into ceramic, fondant, foam, rubber, gummy candy, plastic, resin, glass, or metal.

## Form and Composition

- Simplify objects into broad, instantly recognizable silhouettes.
- Keep manufactured logic where recognition needs it; place imperfection in material surfaces.
- Use slight rotations, uneven heights, and soft overlap without random scattering.
- Preserve generous cream negative space. Clay is a focal moment, not a card treatment.
- No faces, eyes, humans, humanoids, robot characters, or decorative electronics.

## Color Direction

Use Canvas Cream `#FFFAF0`, Ink `#0A0A0A`, and the seven brand hues only. Compose a dominant
family rather than cycling colors randomly. Pink and Coral add energy; quieter Lavender,
Peach, Mint, and Ochre build the field. Teal appears once as the resolved featured signal.
Avoid adjacent repetition and mechanical rainbow checkerboards.

## Camera and Lighting

Use a bright 3/4 top-down product-study camera, normally 25-40 degrees above the cream board.
One large diffuse source from upper-left plus weak cream bounce creates short, gentle contact
shadows. Shadows explain separation and overlap; they never become dramatic. No rim light,
vignette, hard specular highlight, deep depth of field, or cinematic darkness.

## Motion Language

Animate material behavior, not generic UI feedback: slow compression, drag, settling, a small
squash, a retained dent, or a hand-rolled reveal. Use one ordered action sequence with long
rests. Avoid rubbery overshoot, perpetual spinning, hovering, glowing, or constant ambient
motion. End on a complete static poster; reduced motion uses the final frame.

## Global Failure Modes

- A real object with a clay texture filter.
- Perfect procedural geometry or fingerprints applied everywhere.
- Cute characters, branded props, invented text, or decorative symbols.
- Mixed materials inside one illustration.
- Random palette cycling with no focal hierarchy.
- Busy motion that overwhelms the subject's meaning.
