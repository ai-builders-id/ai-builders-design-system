# AI Builders Clay AI-Agent Workshop — Study

This study applies `illustrations-clay.md` to agent-building as an instruction-to-proof
workshop. Subject-specific tension: **complex orchestration made tangible through craft**.

## Study Direction

### Creative Thesis

Show agent building as a sequence of shaping, connecting, inspecting, and approving—not as a
robot character or a magical autonomous machine. The scene is a tactile systems workshop:
software concepts become hand-formed tools and components whose relationships remain clear
without labels.

The central tension is **complex orchestration made approachable through physical craft**.
Objects can be playful, but the composition must communicate purposeful construction from
prompt to completed result.

### Concept Grammar

Each object has one narrative role:

| Role | Object | Visual behavior |
|---|---|---|
| Instruction | Terminal slab | Open, quiet starting surface |
| Orchestration | Gear | Central turn or handoff point |
| Construction | Blocks | Accumulating, offset structure |
| Intervention | Wrench / screwdriver | Diagonal tool crossing one stage |
| Evaluation | Magnifying glass | Focused inspection of a small detail |
| Completion | Checkmark stamp | Stable endpoint with the strongest closure |
| Flow | Putty ribbons | Continuous directional relationship |

Do not add objects unless they carry a distinct role. The workshop should read from
silhouette and placement, not from literal labels or fake UI details.

### Form Language

- Slabs and blocks use softly compressed rectangular forms.
- The gear is chunky with 6-8 irregular but readable teeth; avoid mechanical precision.
- Tools are simplified into broad silhouettes with no sharp or dangerous-looking tips.
- The magnifying glass uses a solid putty disc or shallow inset, never transparent glass.
- The checkmark is thick, grounded, and stamp-like rather than a floating UI icon.
- Ribbons flatten where they contact objects and rise gently between them, proving they are
  soft material rather than cables.

Use asymmetric hand marks selectively. Larger objects receive broad thumb pressure; smaller
objects show pinched edges. Keep the terminal relatively calm so it can act as the starting
visual rest.

### Spatial Composition

Arrange the workshop as a loose left-to-right arc, not a straight assembly line or rigid grid.
The terminal begins in the left third, the gear and blocks form the dense central workshop,
and the checkmark settles in the right third. Tools overlap the central cluster by 10-20%.
Ribbons weave beneath and over objects to clarify sequence.

Use three beats—**instruction, making, proof**—with the middle beat densest. Preserve cream
negative space above and below the arc and a small pause before the final checkmark so
completion feels earned.

### Material Signature

Everything except the cream board is formed from the same family of dense reusable adhesive
putty. Surfaces are matte, faintly waxy in light falloff, and visibly hand-compressed. Marks
are broad and shallow; avoid noisy fingerprints on every piece. Objects retain their own
silhouettes and never slump into a melted pile.

The scene must not drift toward ceramic miniatures, gummy candy, fondant, rubber toys,
plastic icons, or glossy claymorphism.

### Color Direction

Assign color by narrative role rather than random cycling:

- Terminal: Lavender, Mint, or Peach for an inviting start.
- Gear / orchestration: one high-energy Pink, Coral, or Ochre focal piece.
- Blocks: a balanced mix of quieter brand hues.
- Tool and magnifier: contrasting accents that remain secondary.
- Checkmark: Teal as the single resolved signal, or Coral when Teal is already used once.
- Ribbons: colors may change at handoffs, but adjacent ribbon and object colors must differ.

No single hue should dominate more than roughly one third of the object cluster.

### Lighting and Camera

Use a bright 3/4 top-down camera, approximately 30-40 degrees above the board. One large
diffuse source from upper-left creates short, gentle contact shadows; cream bounce keeps the
dark sides open. Shadow overlap should reveal which ribbons pass under which objects. Avoid
deep depth of field, vignette, spotlighting, rim lights, and cinematic darkness.

### Motion Direction

Motion reveals orchestration one stage at a time:

1. The terminal's blank input ridge presses inward slightly.
2. The first ribbon grows toward the gear with a hand-rolled leading edge.
3. The gear turns 20-35 degrees with mild material drag, not a perfect loop.
4. Blocks settle into place one at a time with a small squash.
5. The magnifier tilts toward the final block; the tool makes one restrained adjustment.
6. The last ribbon reaches the checkmark, which presses down and leaves a shallow impression.

Run the sequence in 6-10 seconds, then hold the completed workshop. If looped, use a long
pause and a soft reset rather than reversing every action mechanically. No perpetual gear
spin, elastic bouncing, hovering, glowing paths, or autonomous character behavior. Reduced
motion shows the completed static arrangement.

### Failure Modes

- A cute robot-building scene or anthropomorphic agent mascot.
- Generic clay icons scattered without a visible instruction-to-proof sequence.
- A rigid technical flowchart with equal spacing and no material overlap.
- Transparent magnifying glass, metallic tools, plastic blocks, or glowing connectors.
- Too many props, causing the workshop metaphor to become a cluttered desk.
- Fingerprint noise on every surface or deformation that destroys object recognition.
- Continuous busy motion that makes orchestration feel chaotic rather than deliberate.

## Production Prompt

Create a premium AI Builders illustration: a "building AI agents" workshop scene, made
entirely of matte kneaded putty / plasticine objects arranged on a cream board.

Reference direction: plasticine texture close up, blu tack fidget art, soft clay 3D icon
design. Every object in the scene should look hand-kneaded from adhesive putty — matte,
faintly waxy, asymmetric, with visible fingerprint/thumbprint dents on several pieces, as if
just shaped by hand and set down. Not glossy, not glass, not metal.

Compose the scene from these inanimate objects, arranged as a loose workshop layout with
soft overlap and slight scattering (not a rigid grid):

- A terminal/prompt-window slab with a blank horizontal input line ridge (no visible text)
- A gear/cog shape, mid-size, resting near the terminal as if mid-turn
- A small stack of building blocks/cubes, slightly offset from each other, suggesting
  construction in progress
- A wrench or screwdriver shape resting across or near the blocks
- A magnifying glass shape angled over one of the smaller pieces
- A checkmark-stamp shape near the "finished" end of the composition
- 2-3 ribbon-like putty connector strips linking the terminal to the gear, the gear to the
  blocks, and the blocks toward the checkmark stamp, suggesting a left-to-right flow from
  instruction to finished agent

Do not include any human figures, hands, faces, eyes, or character features on any object —
this is a still-life of tools and shapes only, with no humanoid or robot elements. Do not
include circuit boards, electrical cables, plugs, or any hardware/electronics imagery — the
connector strips must read as soft putty ribbon, never as wiring.

Material and lighting: matte kneaded-putty finish throughout — no specular highlights, no
reflections, no transparency, no glossy sheen. A faint waxy softness in how light falls off
at rounded edges is fine, but it must never read as shine. Lighting is soft, diffuse, and
low-contrast, like a studio softbox — shadows are gentle, never dramatic, and should subtly
reveal the fingerprint dents without dramatizing them.

Camera: bright 3/4 top-down view, objects readable at a glance, laid out left to right so the
"flow" from terminal to checkmark reads naturally.

Palette — use only these colors: Canvas Cream #FFFAF0 as the board/background; Ink #0A0A0A
for the darkest shadow accents only; and objects colored from Brand Pink #FF4D8B, Brand Teal
#1A3A3A (use sparingly, 1 object max), Brand Lavender #B8A4ED, Brand Peach #FFB084, Brand
Ochre #E8B94A, Brand Mint #A4D4C5, and Brand Coral #FF6B5A. Assign color by narrative role:
use a calm Lavender, Mint, or Peach terminal; a high-energy Pink, Coral, or Ochre gear;
balanced quieter blocks; contrasting secondary tools; and Teal as the single resolved
checkmark signal. Keep adjacent pieces distinct and do not introduce any hue outside this set.

No visible text, labels, numbers, or decorative symbols on any object surface. The required
checkmark form and its shallow stamped impression are the only symbol exception. This is a
material/concept study, not a labeled diagram.

Avoid: glass or resin transparency, chrome or painted metal, hard specular highlights,
low-poly faceted look, humanoid or robot figures of any kind, circuit boards, electrical
wiring, real logos, watermarks, any visible text or branding, botanical decoration, neon or
cyberpunk colors, cluttered or overcrowded composition — leave visible cream negative space
around the object cluster.

Output: 1600x900px, 16:9 aspect ratio, wide illustration format. Spread the objects across
the width with generous cream margin top and bottom.
