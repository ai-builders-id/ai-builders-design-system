# AI Builders Clay Keyboard

## Art Direction

### Creative Thesis

The keyboard is a familiar precision tool rebuilt from memory by hand. Its grid remains
immediately recognizable, but every key records pressure, pinching, and tiny decisions made
by a human. The image should feel playful and tactile without becoming toy-like, childish,
or cute.

The central tension is **manufactured layout versus handmade matter**. Preserve enough order
to read “keyboard” instantly, then introduce imperfection through material rather than random
composition.

### Form Language

- Keep the keyboard footprint broad, low, and structurally coherent.
- Keycaps share a family resemblance but are not clones: vary corner softness, top-plane
  slope, and compression subtly.
- Use three deformation types across the board: thumb dents, pinched corners, and gently
  compressed sides. Do not apply all three to every key.
- Approximately 15-25% of keys should carry visible hand marks; the rest stay softly uneven.
- The spacebar is the material hero: larger, calmer in color, and visibly shaped by two or
  three broad presses rather than many noisy fingerprints.
- Maintain consistent gaps. Keys may lean or sit at different heights, but should not melt
  into one continuous slab.

### Material Signature

The clay must read as dense reusable adhesive putty, not ceramic, fondant, foam, rubber, or
plastic. Surfaces are matte with broad soft light falloff. Fingerprints appear as shallow,
partial impressions with softened ridges; never sharp forensic prints. Edges hold their form
but show slight drag and compression where fingers would release the material.

Avoid excessive cracks, crumbs, dust, wet shine, translucent edges, subsurface glow, and
perfect procedural noise. One believable touch mark is more valuable than texture everywhere.

### Composition and Camera

Use a 3/4 top-down product-study view, approximately 25-35 degrees above the board. Keep the
keyboard centered but not clinically symmetrical: a small rotation of 2-5 degrees helps it
feel placed by hand. Preserve generous cream space around the silhouette and avoid props.

The full outline, spacebar, and several key rows must remain legible at thumbnail size. Do
not crop the keyboard merely to create drama; this study is about the object as a complete
system.

### Color Direction

Color behaves like a composed field, not a random rainbow. Establish a dominant family using
Lavender, Peach, Mint, and Ochre across roughly 70% of keys. Pink and Coral provide rhythmic
accents. Teal appears once as the visual anchor, preferably on the spacebar or one intentional
key. Avoid identical adjacent hues, but also avoid a mechanical checkerboard.

### Lighting and Shadow

Use one large diffuse source from the upper-left and a weak cream bounce from the opposite
side. Shadows remain short, warm-neutral, and soft enough to keep the palette bright. Contact
shadows beneath uneven keys are important: they explain height variation and prove that the
pieces are separate. No rim light, spotlight, dramatic vignette, or glossy highlight.

### Motion Direction

When animated, treat motion as stop-motion material behavior rather than interface feedback:

- 3-5 selected keys depress slowly by 6-10% of their height and retain a faint new dent.
- The spacebar may compress once with a broad, weighty squash and imperfect recovery.
- Neighboring keys react by less than 2%, creating subtle physical sympathy.
- Use an irregular 8-12 second loop with long rests; never animate every key.
- No bouncing, rubbery overshoot, typing hands, floating pieces, glow, or sound-dependent cue.
- Reduced motion uses the strongest static material frame.

### Failure Modes

- A real plastic keyboard with a clay texture filter.
- Perfectly repeated key geometry or perfectly aligned rows.
- Rainbow checkerboard color distribution with no focal hierarchy.
- Deep fingerprints on every key, making the surface damaged or grotesque.
- Fondant softness, ceramic polish, glossy toy plastic, or rubber bounce.
- Character faces, alphabet legends, decorative symbols, or branded keys.
- Dramatic studio photography that overwhelms the quiet tactile study.

## Production Prompt

Create a premium AI Builders illustration: a computer keyboard entirely made of matte kneaded
putty / plasticine, in the style of soft clay 3D icon design and Blu Tack fidget art.

Reference direction: plasticine texture close up, blu tack fidget art, soft clay 3D icon
design. The keyboard and every keycap should look hand-kneaded from adhesive putty — matte,
faintly waxy, asymmetric, with visible fingerprint and thumbprint dents pressed into several
keycaps, as if just shaped by hand and set down on the board. Not glossy, not glass, not
metal, not a real plastic keyboard — the material reads as soft, pinchable putty throughout.

Composition: a 3/4 top-down view of a full keyboard, keycaps slightly uneven in height and
alignment (hand-formed, not manufactured-precise), each keycap a distinct rounded, slightly
lumpy block. A few keycaps show one or two faint fingerprint dents on their surface. The
spacebar is the largest piece, visibly pinched into shape with a soft irregular top surface.

Material and lighting: matte kneaded-putty finish — no specular highlights, no reflections,
no transparency, no glossy sheen. A faint waxy softness in how light falls off at the rounded
edges is fine, but it must never read as shine. Lighting is soft, diffuse, and low-contrast,
like a studio softbox — shadows are gentle, never dramatic, and should subtly reveal the
fingerprint dents without dramatizing them.

Palette — use only these colors: Canvas Cream #FFFAF0 as the background/board surface; Ink
#0A0A0A for the darkest shadow accents only; and keycaps colored from Brand Pink #FF4D8B,
Brand Teal #1A3A3A (use exactly once, e.g. the spacebar or a single accent key),
Brand Lavender #B8A4ED, Brand Peach #FFB084, Brand Ochre #E8B94A, Brand Mint #A4D4C5, and
Brand Coral #FF6B5A. Build roughly 70% of the keyboard from Lavender, Peach, Mint, and Ochre;
use Pink and Coral as rhythmic accents and Teal as the single visual anchor. Do not repeat
the same hue on adjacent keys or arrange colors as a mechanical checkerboard. Do not
introduce any hue outside this set.

No visible letters, numbers, or symbols printed on the keycaps — keep every key blank, since
this is a material/texture study, not a functional keyboard render.

Avoid: glass or resin transparency, chrome or painted metal, hard specular highlights,
low-poly faceted look, a perfectly smooth/symmetric/manufactured keyboard look, photorealistic
plastic material, character or mascot features (no eyes, no face on any element), real logos,
watermarks, any visible text or branding, background clutter, botanical decoration, neon or
cyberpunk colors.

Output: 1600x1200px, square-ish product-study crop, keyboard centered with generous cream
negative space around it.
